# Feature Ideas — for coordinator review, not for building yet

Ten-plus proactive ideas for Heliotrope, imagined from her life and
health context (sleep disorder, meds, the doctor run, modafinil
anchored to wake time, cycle, stress, skin, weight, diary, orders
from her inbox). One line + why she'd love it each.

## 1. One Big Thing
Today tab leads with a single glanceable priority (Oura's pattern) —
everything else collapses below. Why she'd love it: she wakes up
disoriented and wants "what I need right now, nothing else," not a
dashboard to parse.

## 2. Body battery / energy forecast
A Whoop-style daily capacity estimate built from sleep + meds +
recent load, so she can plan around actual capacity instead of
discovering she has none at 3pm. Why: she works 8+ hours plus
weekends with no sense of how much she has left in the tank.

## 3. Wake-time med anchoring
Modafinil's dose logic anchors to actual wake time, not clock time —
on a 2pm-wake day the schedule reshapes itself and flags the late-day
question for her prescriber. Why: her standing open question is
exactly skip vs take vs reduced on late-wake days; the app should
encode the rule instead of making her redo the math.

## 4. Interaction checker
Cross-checks her med list against interactions (the modafinil ×
hormonal-birth-control flag she already raised with her doctor is the
poster child), and surfaces new interactions the moment a med is
added. Why: a drug-sensitive user adding a new hormonal med; she
shouldn't have to be her own pharmacist.

## 5. Episode logger
Timestamped log for acute episodes — the dizziness-and-jaw-tension
scare gets start time, symptoms, what she did, what helped — with
a calm checklist built in. Why: she was offered exactly this and the
offer stood unanswered; it needs to be one tap, not a conversation.

## 6. Pattern correlations
CareKit-style charts pairing symptoms against med intake, sleep,
cycle — e.g. nausea vs meds, jaw tension vs stress, fatigue vs
period phase. Why: she asks for the honest pattern read from her own
logged data and forms her own root-cause hypotheses; give her the
raw overlays, not conclusions.

## 7. Twelve-month cycle view
Cycle Insights-style: phase and prediction data from a single night,
shown on a 12-month window, correlated with fatigue and sleep. Why:
she's tracking cycle/fatigue/sleep as one problem, not three.

## 8. Sleep debt & recovery meter
Pulls from the sleep tracker suite: a living sleep-debt meter and
recovery score on the Today tab. Why: she's drowning in raw sleep
data and wants the one-line verdict — restorative or not.

## 9. Refill countdown cards
Each med shows days-remaining, and the card tells her who to call —
one tap to Laurel Springs Publix, prescriber, or insurance. Why: the
README already promises "refill reminders that tell me who to call";
the countdown is what makes it real.

## 10. Insurance card wallet
Her insurance card as a tap-to-show card in Care team, for the
front desk. Why: unglamorous, and exactly the kind of nowhere-to-find
thing this app exists to fix.

## 11. Visit report builder
One-tap doctor-visit summary: labs with trends, current meds,
symptom episodes, open questions — exportable, doctor-ready. Why:
she's mid doctor-run with 7 appointment types still to find; she
should never re-explain her history from scratch again.

## 12. Allergen / trigger diary
Log exposures (pollen, dust, foods) against congestion, sneezing,
fatigue — she already hypothesized untreated allergies as a root
cause. Why: she wants hypotheses falsified with her own evidence;
a trigger diary is the instrument.

## 13. Water ripple tracker
Hydration as a stained-glass water level that fills through the day
— beautiful, no nagging. Why: she likes cold water and beautiful
things; a chore tracker she'd actually open.

## 14. Skin timelapse journal
R2 photo journal for skin: one photo a day, scrub to see change
over weeks, tag products used. Why: skin is one of her upkeep tabs
and photos beat memory for "is this product doing anything."

## 15. Streak constellations
Adherence streaks rendered as Pleiades-style star maps — each night
a star, streaks forming constellations she names. Why: her sleep
streak is at 13 nights and her avatar has the Pleiades on its back;
make the streak itself the art.

## 16. Panic-safe mode
One tap turns the app into a calm screen: sit-or-lie-down guidance,
breathing pacer, red-flag checklist, "someone is home?" prompt. Why:
her acute scare protocol puts safety first, then patterns — the app
should know that order too.

## 17. Order radar
Scans her inbox for health shipments (vitamins, refills, devices)
and shows a tracking timeline in Orders. Why: meds are "incoming"
half the time and she forgets what's already on the way.

## 18. Mood weather map
Diary moods rendered as sky conditions — clear, overcast, stormy —
over a month, so patterns read at a glance. Why: she loves the sky
and wanted "patterns from my days and diary, not diagnoses"; weather
is the honest, non-clinical visual language.
