## What it's for

A mineral supplement. People commonly take it for muscle function, general relaxation, and evening wind-down. Note the honest version: magnesium is **not a proven sleeping pill** — if it helps you unwind, that's a personal response, not a clinical guarantee.

## How and when to take it

- **Read the label for *elemental* magnesium**, not the total compound weight — "500 mg magnesium citrate" is not 500 mg of magnesium. The elemental number is the dose that matters.
- **With food if your stomach complains.** Some forms (oxide, citrate, chloride) are more likely to cause diarrhea, nausea, or cramps; taking them with a meal usually settles this. Glycinate and threonate tend to be gentler.
- **Evening is the common choice** if you're taking it to wind down — but there's no label requirement; consistency beats timing.
- **The spacing rules (important when iron is taken too):** magnesium binds to several things in the gut and blocks their absorption. Keep magnesium at least **2 hours apart from**: iron supplements, calcium, zinc, thyroid medicine, and the antibiotics tetracycline and fluoroquinolones (for antibiotics, the rule is usually 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after the antibiotic). Bisphosphonates (bone medicines): at least 2 hours apart.
- **Kidney disease:** the kidneys clear excess magnesium. If you have reduced kidney function, high doses can build up to dangerous levels — talk to your doctor before supplementing.

## Storage

- Room temperature, dry place, tightly closed. **Not the bathroom** (moisture makes tablets crumble and powders clump).
- Powder forms: keep the desiccant packet in the tub, reseal promptly.
- Keep out of reach of children and pets.

## What to avoid

- **Don't chase the dose upward** if one dose "does nothing" — the most common side effect of too much magnesium is diarrhea, which is your body telling you to back off. Ask your pharmacist before increasing.
- High-dose zinc and high-dose magnesium compete; don't stack large mineral doses without a plan.
- Combining with blood-pressure medicines can add up to lower-than-intended blood pressure — mention it to your prescriber.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember, or just skip to the next day. **Don't double up.**
- Magnesium is a daily-habit supplement, not a timed medicine — one missed day doesn't matter.
