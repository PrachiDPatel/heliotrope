## What it's for

A prescription antihistamine with calming and anti-itch effects. Common uses: anxiety, itching/allergic skin reactions, and pre-procedure sedation. It is **not** a controlled substance, but it is strongly sedating — a single dose can affect some people well into the next day. That is exactly why the dosing rules below matter.

## How and when to take it

- **Exactly as prescribed** — dose, frequency, and purpose all come from the prescriber, not the card. Don't restart an old prescription on your own; prescriptions expire for a reason, and your situation may have changed since it was written.
- **With or without food.**
- Because of her known sensitivity: the standard advice — start only as directed, don't combine with other sedating things, and plan for drowsiness — applies double.

## Storage

- Room temperature (20–25 °C / 68–77 °F), in the original labeled container, away from moisture and heat. **Not the bathroom.**
- Check the expiry date before any future use. If it's expired, don't take it — ask the pharmacist about disposal (take-back programs, not the trash or toilet).

## What to avoid

- **Alcohol — no.** Alcohol stacks with hydroxyzine's sedation: stronger drowsiness, worse thinking and judgment, impaired coordination. Same for other sedatives: sleep aids, opioids, muscle relaxants, other antihistamines. Check with your doctor before combining any of these.
- **Don't drive or do anything risky until you know how it affects you.** Given her history with this drug, assume the first dose needs a clear schedule.
- **Heart rhythm:** hydroxyzine can prolong the QT interval. Tell your doctor about any heart rhythm history (personal or family), and about other QT-prolonging medicines — certain antibiotics (azithromycin, erythromycin), some antidepressants (citalopram, fluoxetine), and some antipsychotics. Your pharmacist can screen the whole list.
- **Serious effects — seek care:** fast/pounding/uneven heartbeat, fainting, fever with skin rash (rare serious skin reaction AGEP is a labeled warning), trouble urinating, severe confusion.
- Common effects: drowsiness, dizziness, dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary hesitancy.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's close to the next dose. Then skip.
- **Don't double up.**
- If it's a scheduled (not as-needed) prescription and you miss often, tell the prescriber.
