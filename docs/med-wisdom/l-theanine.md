## What it's for

An amino acid found naturally in green tea. People commonly take it for calm focus — relaxation without sedation. It's why a cup of green tea feels different from a cup of coffee. Studies typically use 100–200 mg; the FDA lists it as Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS), and studies have used up to 400–900 mg/day without serious adverse effects.

## How and when to take it

- **With or without food.** Taking it on an empty stomach can occasionally cause mild stomach discomfort — with food if that happens.
- **Common pattern:** people take it during the day for focus or in the evening to wind down; effects typically come on in 30–60 minutes and last a few hours. Product labels commonly list 50–200 mg per serving — follow the product's label; published studies have typically used 100–200 mg.
- **It is not a sedative.** It promotes relaxation without drowsiness — don't expect it to knock you out, and don't take more expecting a stronger "sleep" effect.

## Storage

- **Cool, dry, dark place** — a pantry or bedroom cabinet. Not the windowsill, not next to a steamy shower, not the bathroom.
- Keep the bottle tightly closed; moisture is the enemy of capsules and powders.
- Keep out of reach of children and pets.

## What to avoid / be aware of

- **Blood pressure:** l-theanine can modestly lower blood pressure. If you take blood-pressure medicines, or run low already, mention it to your prescriber — the effects can add up.
- **Sedatives:** it may enhance the effects of sedative medicines (benzodiazepines and similar). Tell your pharmacist everything you take.
- **Stimulants:** it's often paired with caffeine, but combining with prescription stimulants (including modafinil) is a pharmacist conversation, not a DIY stack — ask before combining.
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** limited safety data — generally advised to avoid unless your doctor says otherwise.
- **Supplement quality:** the FDA doesn't regulate supplements the way it regulates medicines. Look for third-party testing (USP, NSF) on the label.
- Rare mild side effects: headache, dizziness, mild GI discomfort — more likely at high doses.

## If you miss a dose

- Just take it next time you think of it, or skip to tomorrow. **Don't double up.**
- L-theanine is an as-needed/daily-habit supplement, not a timed medicine — missing a dose has no consequence beyond missing the effect.
