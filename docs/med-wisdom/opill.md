# Opill (norgestrel 0.075 mg) — practical wisdom card

> **Informational only — not medical advice.** This card summarizes the FDA Drug Facts label and standard references for Opill. It is not a recommendation to start, stop, or change anything. **Ask your pharmacist or doctor** about anything here.

> *Context: the FDA-labeled indication is prevention of pregnancy. Some people also track cycle-related mood symptoms alongside it — the timing rules below matter regardless of purpose, since the pill works by keeping a steady hormone level.*

## What it's for

A progestin-only ("minipill") daily oral contraceptive, available over the counter. One tablet (0.075 mg norgestrel) every day. It does **not** protect against STIs.

## How and when to take it — the 3-hour rule is everything

- **Same time every day, within a 3-hour window.** Pick a time you'll actually hit (e.g., 8:00 AM means between 8:00 and 11:00 AM). The minipill's effect on cervical mucus is short-lived, so strict timing is what keeps it working — this is stricter than combination pills.
- **With or without food.** Swallow whole with water.
- **Starting:** can start any day. If you start on day 1 of your period, no backup needed; any other day, use backup (condoms) for the first 48 hours.
- **When you finish a 28-tablet pack, start the next pack the next day** — no break week.
- **Vomiting or severe diarrhea within 4 hours of the pill:** treat it as a missed pill (the dose may not have been absorbed). Use backup for 48 hours after the episode resolves; keep taking the daily pill at the usual time.

## Storage

- Room temperature, dry place. Not the bathroom (humidity), not a hot car.
- Keep in the original pack — the blister layout is your built-in "did I take it today" tracker. A phone alarm at pill time is the standard pharmacist recommendation.
- **Travel:** keep the pack with you (purse/carry-on). If you cross time zones, keep taking it at the same *interval* — ask your pharmacist before a big trip.

## What to avoid

- **Things that make Opill less effective:** certain seizure medicines (phenytoin, carbamazepine, topiramate), tuberculosis medicine (rifampin), some HIV medicines (efavirenz), and bosentan. **Also: modafinil** — it induces the liver enzyme CYP3A4, and the modafinil FDA label says hormonal contraceptives may be less effective **during treatment and for 1 month after stopping modafinil**. If both are taken together, **talk to your prescriber and pharmacist about whether backup contraception is needed.**
- **Common, usually not dangerous:** irregular bleeding/spotting, headaches, dizziness, nausea, increased appetite, cramps, bloating. These often settle over the first few months. Keep taking it daily unless a clinician tells you to stop.
- **Seek care promptly** for: severe abdominal or chest pain, vision changes, severe headache, signs of depression worsening, or a late period after missed pills (take a pregnancy test or talk to a doctor).

## If you miss a dose

- **Less than 3 hours late:** take 1 tablet right away, back to the usual time tomorrow. No backup needed (if you've been taking it correctly).
- **More than 3 hours late, or missed one or more tablets:** take 1 tablet as soon as you remember, then continue at the usual time — this may mean 2 tablets in one day. **Use a condom or other barrier method every time for the next 48 hours**, because it takes about 2 days for Opill to be fully effective again.
- **Never** take extra tablets to "catch up" beyond the one make-up dose.
