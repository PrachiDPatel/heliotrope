## What it's for

A beta blocker. Prescribed uses include high blood pressure, heart rhythm problems, migraine prevention, and situational anxiety (it blunts the physical symptoms — racing heart, shaking). It slows the heart and lowers blood pressure.

## How and when to take it

- **Exactly as prescribed.** Doses are often adjusted several times to find what works — don't self-adjust.
- **With or without food — but the same way each time** (food changes how much gets absorbed, so consistency keeps the dose consistent).
- Extended-release capsules: **swallow whole**; don't crush, break, or chew.

## The one rule that matters most: never stop suddenly

- **Do NOT stop propranolol abruptly.** Stopping suddenly can cause rebound chest pain, heart rhythm problems, or (in people taking it for heart conditions) a heart attack. If it ever needs to stop, the prescriber tapers it down gradually.
- This is the single most important thing to know about this drug — more important than any other section on this card.

## Storage

- Room temperature, closed container, away from heat, moisture, and direct light. **Not the bathroom.** Don't freeze liquid forms.
- Check the expiry before any future use; expired prescriptions shouldn't be restarted without the prescriber.

## What to avoid

- **Alcohol:** additive blood-pressure lowering and drowsiness — dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting, especially when starting or changing dose. Get up slowly from sitting/lying.
- **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen and similar) can reduce propranolol's blood-pressure effect — mention regular use to your prescriber.
- **It can mask low blood sugar symptoms** (like a racing heartbeat) — relevant if you ever track glucose.
- **Tell every doctor and dentist** you take it — it affects some medical tests and anesthesia plans; don't stop before surgery without the prescriber's approval.
- **Interaction note for her list:** modafinil reversibly inhibits the liver enzyme CYP2C19 (the modafinil label names propranolol among drugs whose exposure *may* increase). Propranolol is only partly cleared by CYP2C19 — its main clearance pathways are others — so the real-world significance is uncertain. **A pharmacist should review this pair before propranolol is ever taken alongside modafinil.**
- Common effects: fatigue, dizziness, cold hands/feet, slow pulse, vivid dreams, stomach upset. Seek care for: chest pain, very slow or irregular heartbeat, fainting, wheezing/trouble breathing, swelling.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's almost time for the next dose. Then skip and take the regular dose.
- **Don't take two doses at once.**
- Missing one dose is far less dangerous than stopping outright — but tell the prescriber if misses are frequent.
