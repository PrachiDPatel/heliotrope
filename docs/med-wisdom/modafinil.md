# Modafinil — practical wisdom card

> **Informational only — not medical advice.** This card summarizes pharmacist knowledge from the FDA label and standard drug references. It is not a recommendation to start, stop, or change anything. **Ask your pharmacist or doctor** about anything here, especially before combining with other medications.

## What it's for

Modafinil is a wakefulness-promoting medicine (Schedule IV controlled substance). It's prescribed for excessive daytime sleepiness — in narcolepsy, shift-work sleep disorder, and obstructive sleep apnea. It promotes wakefulness; it isn't a treatment for the underlying sleep condition, and it doesn't replace real sleep or, for sleep apnea, a CPAP.

## How and when to take it

- **Timing:** usually once daily in the **morning**, per your prescriber. Her prescriber's written plan: a fraction of a tablet after breakfast, wait 2 hours, no more than half a tablet on day one, increasing slowly only as directed. Follow *that* plan, not the bottle alone.
- **With or without food?** Either is fine per the label. Note: taking it with food can delay absorption by about an hour — so if it kicks in slowly one day, a big breakfast may be why.
- **Don't take it late in the day.** If taken too close to bedtime, it can make it harder to fall asleep. Anchor it to your wake time, not the clock.
- **Same way each time:** keep the routine consistent so you can actually judge whether it's working.

## Storage

- Room temperature, 20–25 °C (68–77 °F). Cool, dry, dark — a bedroom cabinet, not the bathroom (shower humidity degrades pills).
- Keep in the original container with the label on it.
- **Travel:** keep in original labeled container (it's a controlled substance — carry it as labeled, in carry-on, not checked luggage). Don't leave it in a hot car.
- Because it's Schedule IV: store where others can't access it; never share it with anyone else, even if they have the same symptoms.

## What to avoid

- **Alcohol:** the label advises avoiding alcohol while taking it — it can make side effects worse, and sleepiness already affects alertness on its own.
- **Other stimulants/sedatives:** be cautious combining with other things that act on wakefulness or sleepiness; tell your prescriber everything you take.
- **Hormonal birth control — important:** modafinil induces CYP3A4, a liver enzyme that speeds up the breakdown of steroidal contraceptives. The FDA label says hormonal methods (pill, implant, shot, ring, IUD with hormones) may be less effective **during treatment and for 1 month after stopping**. **Talk to your prescriber/pharmacist about backup contraception.**
- **Driving:** avoid driving or risky activities until you know how it affects you — especially relevant since dizziness and sleepiness are the exact problems being treated.
- **Watch for and report right away:** rash or skin changes (rare but serious skin reactions are a labeled warning), chest pain, signs of depression/anxiety/psychosis, unusual thoughts.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's close to the next dose or late in the day. If it's nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed one.
- **Never take a double dose** to make up for a missed one.
- If you miss doses often, tell your prescriber — the plan may need adjusting.

## Interactions worth knowing about (tell your pharmacist you take these too)

- Modafinil can *slow the clearance* of drugs cleared by CYP2C19 — examples include **propranolol**, omeprazole, diazepam, and some antidepressants. She has propranolol on hand (not currently taking): worth a pharmacist conversation before it ever gets used.
- Warfarin/blood thinners need more monitoring; cyclosporine levels can drop.
