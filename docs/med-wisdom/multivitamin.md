## What it's for

A daily multivitamin is nutritional insurance — a baseline of vitamins and minerals for days when diet doesn't cover everything. It is **not a substitute for food**, and it doesn't fix a specific deficiency the way a targeted supplement (like iron for low iron) does.

## How and when to take it

- **With food and a full glass of water.** Taking it on an empty stomach commonly causes nausea; food fixes that for most people. Morning with breakfast is the usual habit.
- **Read the label before adding anything else.** The single most important multivitamin skill: check whether it already contains **iron** and **vitamin D** before you take separate iron or D on top of it. If separate iron and vitamin D are also taken — and the multivitamin contains them too — that's double-dosing, and too much iron is its own problem. **Show the multivitamin label to your pharmacist alongside your other bottles.**
- **Spacing:** take with food as directed; if it's an iron-containing multi, avoid taking it at the same time as dairy/milk or calcium (per the label) — breakfast dairy may be the reason to pick another meal. Keep it 2–4 hours from tetracycline/fluoroquinolone antibiotics if you're ever prescribed them. Don't take an antacid within 1 hour before or 2 hours after.
- **Don't take two different multis** or stack a multi with single-nutrient megadoses without checking totals — fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) accumulate in the body.

## Storage

- Room temperature, dry place, bottle tightly closed. **Not the bathroom** — moisture degrades B vitamins and vitamin C fastest.
- Gummies: heat turns them into a single blob; keep them cool.
- **Child safety:** iron-containing multivitamins are a leading cause of accidental child poisoning. Keep out of reach of children — locked or high, not the kitchen counter.

## What to avoid

- **More is not better.** Megadosing "just in case" is how people end up with too much vitamin A (liver, vision issues) or vitamin D (high calcium). The label dose is the dose.
- Tell your prescriber you take it: multis can interact with warfarin (vitamin K), thyroid medicine, and some antibiotics.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's almost time for the next day's dose. Then skip.
- **Don't take two at once.** Missing a day of a multivitamin is nutritionally meaningless.
