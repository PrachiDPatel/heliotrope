# Vitamin D (D3) — practical wisdom card

> **Informational only — not medical advice.** This card summarizes pharmacist knowledge from standard references (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements). It is not a recommendation to start, stop, or change your dose. **Ask your pharmacist or doctor** about anything here — especially the dose, since vitamin D is one vitamin where more is not better.

## What it's for

Vitamin D supports calcium absorption and bone health; it's made by skin in sunlight, and many people run low with indoor routines. Common reasons people supplement: low blood levels, limited sun, bone health.

## How and when to take it

- **Take with a meal that contains some fat.** Vitamin D is fat-soluble — food (especially with fat) meaningfully improves absorption. No particular time of day required; pick a meal you'll remember.
- **Take the dose on the label — don't freelance the dose.** Adult upper limit is 4,000 IU/day (NIH). Vitamin D is stored in the body, and taking too much over time can cause toxicity (high calcium: nausea, weakness, frequent urination, kidney stones). "Higher is not better for bone" — in a 3-year trial, 4,000–10,000 IU/day gave *lower* wrist bone density than 400 IU.
- If your bottle is D3 + K2, note that vitamin K interacts with **warfarin** — keep vitamin K intake steady and tell your prescriber.

## Storage

- Room temperature, dry place, away from heat and direct light. **Not the bathroom.**
- Keep in the original bottle with the lid tightly closed (moisture degrades softgels and tablets alike).
- Keep out of reach of children and pets.

## What to avoid / be aware of

- **Don't megadose.** Stick to the label or your prescriber's dose; toxicity builds quietly over weeks.
- **Thiazide diuretics + vitamin D** can push calcium too high (hypercalcemia risk) — tell your prescriber if you take both.
- **Orlistat and some cholesterol medicines** can reduce vitamin D absorption — space as your pharmacist advises.
- **Magnesium-containing antacids + vitamin D** can raise magnesium levels in kidney disease — worth mentioning to your doctor if relevant.
- Symptoms of too much (over weeks of excess): unusual thirst, frequent urination, nausea, constipation, confusion. Seek care if these appear.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's almost time for the next dose. Then skip.
- **Don't double up.** Vitamin D is stored in the body, so one missed day barely matters; consistency over weeks is what counts.

---

*Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (RDA 600 IU, upper limit 4,000 IU, fat-soluble absorption); Burt LA et al., JAMA 2019;322(8):736–745 — 3-year randomized trial, 400 vs 4,000 vs 10,000 IU/day vitamin D3 (n=311 healthy adults 55–70); higher doses associated with lower radial volumetric BMD; no significant difference in bone strength; authors frame as hypothesis-generating, not proof of harm; MedlinePlus vitamin D drug information (interactions, toxicity); MSKCC patient education (storage, missed dose).*

**If in doubt about anything on this card — ask your pharmacist or doctor.**
