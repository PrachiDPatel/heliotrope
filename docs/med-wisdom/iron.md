# Iron (ferrous sulfate, 65 mg elemental) — practical wisdom card

> **Informational only — not medical advice.** This card summarizes pharmacist knowledge from standard drug references. It is not a recommendation to start, stop, or change anything. **Ask your pharmacist or doctor** about anything here.
>

## What it's for

An iron supplement, taken for low iron (low iron saturation). Iron carries oxygen in the blood; deficiency is a common cause of fatigue, brain fog, and dizziness.

## How and when to take it

- **Best absorption: on an empty stomach** — at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal. Empty stomach = noticeably better uptake.
- **If it upsets your stomach** (very common): it's okay to take it with a small amount of food. Don't quietly quit over nausea — ask your prescriber about alternatives like alternate-day dosing or a different formulation. Side effects are the #1 reason people abandon iron early.
- **The iron spacing rules (the important part):** keep iron **1–2 hours away from** tea, coffee, calcium/dairy, eggs, soy, spinach, and cereals — all of these reduce absorption. This includes her **magnesium** and any **multivitamin**: space iron and magnesium at least 2 hours apart, and don't stack a second iron-containing multivitamin on top without checking with your pharmacist.
- **Vitamin C is optional but helps:** taking iron with orange juice or a vitamin C source can improve absorption. (Her Nature Made iron: check whether the label already includes vitamin C.)
- **Other spacing:** keep iron at least 2 hours from thyroid medicine, certain antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones), antacids, and PPIs.
- **Swallow tablets whole** — don't crush or chew. Full glass of water.
- **Be patient:** it typically takes weeks to months to rebuild iron stores; labs, not feelings, judge progress.

## Storage

- Room temperature, dry place, away from heat and moisture. **Not the bathroom.**
- Keep in the original container, lid tight.
- **Child safety (serious):** iron overdose can be **fatal to a young child**. Keep out of reach of children and pets. This is one of the most important storage warnings on the label — treat it as such.
- **Travel:** fine in a pill organizer for the week; keep the rest sealed in the original bottle.

## What to avoid

- **Don't double up** on iron from multiple sources (iron pill + iron-containing multivitamin) without asking — too much iron is its own problem.
- Avoid taking it at the same time as calcium, dairy, tea, or coffee (see spacing above).
- **Dark or green stools are normal** on iron — expected, not alarming. Constipation, nausea, and stomach cramps are the common side effects.
- **Call your doctor promptly** for: severe stomach pain or vomiting, vomit that looks like coffee grounds, bloody or tarry stools, fever.

## If you miss a dose

- Take it when you remember — **unless** it's almost time for the next dose. Then skip and go back to the normal schedule.
- **Don't take two doses at once.**
- Missing one dose of iron is not an emergency; consistency over weeks matters more than any single day.
